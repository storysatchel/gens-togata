# Image Layout Terminology in Publishing

## Three Primary Types

### Full-Bleed
Image or graphic extends to the very edge of the page with no margin. Requires a "bleed" allowance (typically 0.125") beyond the trim line so the printer can trim to the final size without white edges. Used for dramatic, immersive visuals.

### Vignette
A half-page illustration with a background, typically boxed or with soft, fading edges. Occupies roughly half the page area, leaving space for text or other content. The term historically implies soft or blurred edges but in book design specifically refers to half-page layouts.

### Spot (Spot Art / Spot Illustration)
Small illustration placed within the text column at the flow of the text. No text wrapping by default — the image sits on its own line within the column. Sometimes called "inline" when truly on the baseline with text, but "spot" is the broader, more established publishing term.

## Additional Terms

- **Full-page illustration**: Occupies the entire page (full-bleed or not)
- **Facing page / spread**: Two pages viewed together (left and right)
- **Gutter**: The inner margin where pages meet at the spine
- **Margin**: Empty space around the text block
- **Text wrap**: Text flows around an image rather than above/below
